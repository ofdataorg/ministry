The Ministry of Data is a review desk for open datasets. We download them, use them properly, and
publish what we find — scored on [six axes](/method/), argued in full, and never a number without
its working.

## Why this exists

Open data catalogues are enormous and almost entirely unreviewed. A dataset appears on a portal with
a title, a file size and an update timestamp, and everything a user actually needs to know — whether
the coverage is even, whether the licence permits their use, whether it will join to anything,
whether anyone is still maintaining it — has to be discovered by downloading it and finding out.

Every analyst rediscovers the same problems independently, and none of that knowledge is written
down anywhere durable. That is the gap this site exists to fill.

## What we cover

Datasets from anywhere in the world, in any domain, provided they are open. We deliberately spread
coverage across regions rather than concentrating on the well-served parts of the catalogue —
partly because the under-reviewed datasets are where a review is worth most, and partly because
[the gaps themselves are a finding](/analyses/where-the-map-runs-out/).

Two kinds of thing get published here:

- **Evaluations** — one dataset, six scores, a spec sheet and a verdict.
- **Analyses** — longer pieces that cut across the catalogue: patterns in licensing, coverage,
  freshness and publishing practice.

## Independence

We take no money from any publisher we evaluate. There are no sponsored entries, no paid placement,
and no advance review of drafts by the organisations being assessed. If that ever changes, it will
be stated at the top of every affected page rather than in a policy document nobody reads.

Scores are revisable. When a publisher fixes something, we re-evaluate and date the change.

## Reuse

Everything written here is CC BY 4.0. Quote it, translate it, argue with it in print. If we are
wrong about a dataset, the fastest way to fix that is to [tell us](/submit/).
